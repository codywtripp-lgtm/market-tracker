How this works

Quality is what to expect this time of year: new crop and peak harvest rate excellent; storage, imports, end-of-season and growing-area changeovers rate fair or poor. Tap or hover over a rating for the reason. A low price on poor-quality produce is never listed as a best buy.

Every week USDA records the produce prices U.S. supermarkets advertise, from about 9,300 stores for the most common items. We compare this week’s average price with the same weeks in the last five to ten years, adjusted for inflation: the cheapest quarter of those weeks is a great price, the highest quarter is pricey. The two-week outlook comes from a model of each item’s seasonal pattern and how far it is from its usual price. In testing on 2022 to 2026 prices, the price moved the way the outlook said (by 5% or more) 78% of the time. “In season” is from USDA’s seasonal produce guide. These are advertised prices; your store’s everyday shelf price may differ.
